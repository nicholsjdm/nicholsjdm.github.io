# Reflection

What brings me peace is not the diploma itself. It is the proof that I can get through the hardest seasons of my life and come out the other side still building. 
Fire investigation, a second failed marriage, raising my kids from a distance, working full-time, earning certifications, and now finishing this degree. All of it taught me that I can take a risk and survive the outcome, even when it is bad. 
That confidence is what gives me peace. When I lack peace, it is usually because I do not trust my own decisions or I am afraid to risk. Faith has taught me that Heavenly Father does not leave me. Through the scriptures I find consolation and answers, and even when I do not have answers, I know I will be okay. 
That trust is the foundation under everything else.

Professionally, the same faith shows up in how I handle technology. The field is overwhelming, and AI and new tools can create real anxiety. But technology is a tool and a discipline, not a master. 
There will always be people who use it for deception and harm, and my side is honesty, agency, and reducing harm. I do not have to know everything on day one. I can learn slowly, make mistakes in a lab, and keep moving forward with good mentors and a community that shares my values. 
That is how I connect my spiritual growth to my professional life: the same confidence that carried me through hard personal seasons is the confidence I bring to every ticket, every playbook, and every decision about what I will and will not do with the tools in my hands.

Elder Bednar’s image of strands becoming one rope is the point. Doctrine, principle, and practice are not three lives. They are one person. 
The quiet hour that produced this page is the same work as isolating a host early or writing a playbook other people can follow.

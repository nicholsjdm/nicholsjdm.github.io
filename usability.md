# Usability testing and design

## How I tested
I used the live site at https://nicholsjdm.github.io to check functionality and errors in my writing. I primarily used my desktop browser and mobile phone to check the orientation in portrait. 
I used graded feedback from my instructor during Week 3. He had to open a page and then back out to reach the next one, so I made a left navigation bar for quick and easy browsing.

## What I found
The old layout of the site made every page a dead end. You would have to hit Back and pick again every time you finished reading a page. That made the site feel smaller and more annoying to continue to use. 

I discovered on my phone that the sidebar sits at the top. When I scroll on the page into the writing it moves off the screen. I can still reach Home and the other sections by scrolling up. That did not get in the way of reading, so I left it.

Links use normal words instead of filenames. Pages are text-first, so they stay readable without a heavy theme.

## What I changed
I added a custom layout with a left menu grouped into Professional Growth and Spiritual Progression. Home sits above those groups. I added a Playbook refinement page, Attributes and warning signs page, and Portfolio review page as the project requirement grew so new work is not buried.

On the attributes page I added one of my favorite paintings; Ivan Kramskoy’s  *Christ in the Wilderness* (1872, public domain). I changed the original size of the file so it would look good on the site, adding alt text and a caption. This fulfills my multimedia element for this week.

I kept the full playbook off the public site because a public collection script is not a usability improvement for someone browsing this portfolio.

## Impact
A reader can go from section to section quickly and easily. The phone check confirmed the pages still read on mobile devices. Accessibility is basic**:** such as real headings, real links, alt text on the painting. If I add more images they will get the same treatment.

## Later changes
On 5 October 2026 I switched the site to a dark theme after a classmate said the gray intro text was hard to read. 
Body text is off-white on a dark background so the contrast goes up, not down. 

I also added one image each on Principles, Ethics, Reflection, and Goals, with alt text and a caption, so the long pages are not a wall of text.

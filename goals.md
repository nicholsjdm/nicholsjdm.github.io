# Alignment with my initial goals

In Week 1, I wrote three goals for this course. They still hold and what changed over time is that I stopped treating them like a new project I had to invent.

![A handheld compass](images/compass.jpg)

*The Week 1 goals did not get replaced. They got a direction. Photo: Wikimedia Commons, Compass, 10 August 2026.*

## What I said in Week 1

I would finish a professional ePortfolio by Week 6 with a resume, a home-lab write-up, one sanitized incident-response case, and a skills page. I would put one redacted case in it and say what I refused to assume. I would name three spiritual principles and return to them in hard moments: truth over verdict, consecrated skill, and power under covenant.

Those course goals support the main goal of my life. I want the degree to help me get hired into a cybersecurity analyst role closer to my children’s home. I did not want a portfolio that only listed certificates.

## What I actually built

The site is the portfolio. It has the resume, skills, and projects. It also has an incident response case I led in August 2026, sanitized, with the ethical choice written out instead of the indicators. The evidence playbook came out of that case after I saw how long the work took and that the team did not have a repeatable way to collect evidence. I was already doing that work before Week 1. The class gave me a place to show it.

The three principles did not change. They got sharper because I tied them to real tickets.

Truth over verdict means I gather the facts and tell that story, not the story already in my head. That showed up in the evidence collection, and it shows up on ordinary support tickets when I am tempted to decide what happened before I look.

Consecrated skill means the skill is for serving the person, not for proving I am smart. Users will say they do not know, or they will be frustrated. I do not take that personally. I was trained in this. They were trained in something else. We work the problem together.

Power under covenant means I limit myself. I have tools and access that could be used for selfish reasons or to cause harm. Just because I can does not mean I should. The limit protects the customer, the company’s name, and my own soul.

Matthew 4 is Christ in the wilderness refusing three temptations. I used that story to connect the principles and drew a mental map. In my work the three offers look like this: the paycheck as the highest priority, proving my worth by making someone else feel small, and using access to data as if I were allowed to rearrange someone else’s life. Seeing the temptation is half of it. The class gave me a way to name it and a way to correct when I start to lean.

## What I will use this for

Anyone, including a hiring manager, can open the site and see the work and the person doing it. I will link it on applications. It stays a living record after the degree. Next is PenTest+, the Air National Guard packet, the home lab, and more sanitized lessons from incidents. My mission statement does not change: protect agency and reduce harm.
